background: |+
  Infective diarrhoea is very common; most cases are self-limiting and need only fluids. The skill is spotting **who is sick, who is at risk of a complication, and who needs public health action**. *C. difficile* is covered in the Healthcare-associated infections card.

  - **Viral**: **norovirus** (commonest cause of outbreaks, explosive vomiting and diarrhoea, highly infectious), rotavirus (children; reduced by vaccination), adenovirus.
  - **Bacterial**: ***Campylobacter jejuni*** (**commonest bacterial cause in the UK**; poultry), non-typhoidal ***Salmonella*** (eggs, poultry), ***Shigella*** (person-to-person, men who have sex with men, travel), **Shiga toxin-producing *E. coli* (STEC/EHEC, including O157:H7)** (undercooked beef, raw vegetables; risk of HUS), ETEC (travellers' diarrhoea), ***Vibrio cholerae***, *Yersinia*, and **preformed toxin food poisoning** (*S. aureus*, *B. cereus*: vomiting within 1–6 hours of eating).
  - **Protozoal**: ***Giardia*** (chronic diarrhoea, bloating, steatorrhoea; water, travel), ***Entamoeba histolytica*** (dysentery and liver abscess), ***Cryptosporidium*** (water; severe and prolonged in immunosuppression).
  - **Clues from the history**: incubation period; vomiting-predominant vs diarrhoea-predominant; bloody stool (invasive/cytotoxic); recent antibiotics or hospitalisation (*C. difficile*); travel; outbreak or shared meal; immunosuppression.

  > **Microbiology and pathology**
  >
  > - **Secretory (non-inflammatory) diarrhoea**: toxins increase intestinal secretion without invading. **Cholera toxin** ADP-ribosylates the Gsα subunit → permanent adenylate cyclase activation → ↑cAMP → CFTR chloride secretion with massive isotonic **"rice-water" stool**. ETEC heat-labile toxin acts the same way; heat-stable toxin acts via cGMP.
  > - **Inflammatory/invasive diarrhoea** (bloody, fever): ***Shigella* invades colonic epithelium via M cells**, spreads cell to cell by actin polymerisation (IcsA), and produces Shiga toxin. **STEC** produces **Shiga toxin (Stx)**, which inhibits the 60S ribosomal subunit and damages endothelial cells (renal glomeruli), causing **HUS** (microangiopathic haemolytic anaemia, thrombocytopenia, AKI). *Campylobacter* and *Salmonella* invade the distal ileum and colon with neutrophilic inflammation.
  > - **Norovirus** infects small bowel enterocytes, causing villus blunting and malabsorption; the very low infectious dose (~20 particles), prolonged surface survival and resistance to alcohol gel make outbreaks hard to control. **Immunity is short-lived**.
  > - ***Giardia*** trophozoites attach to the duodenal/jejunal surface and cause brush-border disruption and malabsorption; ***E. histolytica*** invades the colonic mucosa (flask-shaped ulcers) and can reach the liver via the portal vein.
diagnosis: |+
  - **Watery diarrhoea ± vomiting** (viral, toxin-mediated, ETEC, cholera) vs **dysentery** (bloody stool, fever, tenesmus: *Shigella*, *Campylobacter*, STEC, *Salmonella*, *Entamoeba*).
  - **Assess hydration first**: thirst, dry mucosa, postural drop, tachycardia, reduced urine output, confusion in older patients; elderly and frail patients decompensate fast.
  - **Red-flag features**: severe or persistent (>7 days) diarrhoea, blood in the stool, high fever, significant abdominal pain/distension (consider toxic megacolon, appendicitis, ischaemia), immunosuppression, recent travel or antibiotics, and sepsis.
  - **Complications**: dehydration and AKI, electrolyte disturbance, **HUS** (especially STEC in children and older adults, 5–10 days after diarrhoea begins), **reactive arthritis** (*Salmonella*, *Shigella*, *Campylobacter*, *Yersinia*), **Guillain–Barré syndrome** (*Campylobacter*), post-infectious IBS, erythema nodosum (*Yersinia*).
investigations: |+
  - **Stool culture and PCR panel** if: severe illness, **bloody stool**, immunocompromised, recent travel, recent antibiotics or hospital stay, persistent diarrhoea, suspected outbreak, or a food handler/healthcare/childcare worker. Add *C. difficile* testing if risk factors. **Stool microscopy for ova, cysts and parasites** after travel or for persistent diarrhoea (*Giardia*, *Cryptosporidium*, *Entamoeba*).
  - **FBC, U&E, CRP, lactate**; **blood cultures** if febrile or septic (*Salmonella*, *Shigella* and *Campylobacter* can be invasive). **STEC**: monitor **FBC, film, U&E daily** for HUS (falling platelets and Hb, rising creatinine).
  - **Imaging** (abdominal X-ray/CT) only if suspecting an alternative surgical diagnosis, toxic megacolon or perforation.
treatment: |+
  - **Fluids are the treatment.** **Oral rehydration solution (ORS)** for most; **IV fluids** for severe dehydration, shock or persistent vomiting. Replace electrolytes, and continue feeding.
  - **Antimotility agents (loperamide)** only for mild non-bloody diarrhoea without fever; **avoid in bloody or febrile diarrhoea**, suspected STEC, and *C. difficile*. Antiemetics for vomiting.
  - **Antibiotics are not routine.**
    - **Non-typhoidal *Salmonella***: don't treat uncomplicated disease (prolongs carriage); treat **invasive** disease, bacteraemia, the very young/old and the immunosuppressed (for example ciprofloxacin or azithromycin/ceftriaxone by sensitivities).
    - ***Campylobacter***: **azithromycin or clarithromycin** if severe, prolonged or in the immunosuppressed (early treatment shortens illness).
    - ***Shigella***: treat according to sensitivities (azithromycin, ciprofloxacin; resistance is rising).
    - **STEC/EHEC: no antibiotics** (may increase HUS risk); supportive care, nephrology input for HUS.
    - **Cholera**: aggressive rehydration plus azithromycin or doxycycline.
    - **Travellers' diarrhoea**: usually fluids alone; **azithromycin** for severe or dysenteric illness.
  - **Giardia**: tinidazole (single dose) or metronidazole. **Amoebic dysentery/liver abscess**: metronidazole followed by a luminal agent (paromomycin or diloxanide). **Cryptosporidium**: supportive; restore immunity in HIV.
  - **Public health**: many causes are **notifiable** (food poisoning, and specific organisms). **Exclusion**: food handlers, healthcare, childcare and care-home workers until **48 hours after the last symptom** (norovirus and most others), with **microbiological clearance** for enteric group pathogens (typhoid, STEC, *Shigella*). **Handwashing with soap and water**; alcohol gel doesn't inactivate norovirus or *C. difficile* spores. Report outbreaks to the health protection team.
protocols: |+
  > Red flag: bloody diarrhoea followed by **falling platelets, anaemia, and rising creatinine** is **HUS** until proven otherwise — urgent nephrology/haematology input, avoid antibiotics, anti-motility drugs and NSAIDs, and manage fluids carefully.

  - **Severe dehydration or sepsis** from gastroenteritis (especially in older people): resuscitate (see the Fluid therapy card, Acute medicine) and start sepsis management if indicated.
  - **Acute abdomen** (severe pain, peritonism, distension) in a patient with "gastroenteritis": exclude appendicitis, ischaemia, obstruction, perforation and toxic megacolon; involve surgeons.
  - **Immunocompromised patients with prolonged diarrhoea**: always send stool for *Cryptosporidium*, microsporidia, *C. difficile*, CMV colitis consideration (see the Herpesviruses card).
