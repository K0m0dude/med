background: |+
  Eight human herpesviruses share **lifelong latency**, reactivation under immune stress or immunosuppression, and a characteristic sensitivity to nucleoside analogue antivirals (except where stated).

  - **HSV-1 and HSV-2**: orolabial and genital herpes, keratitis, **encephalitis** (HSV-1 mainly), meningitis (HSV-2), **neonatal herpes**, eczema herpeticum.
  - **VZV (HHV-3)**: **chickenpox** (primary infection) and **shingles** (reactivation from dorsal root/cranial nerve ganglia), with complications such as postherpetic neuralgia, **herpes zoster ophthalmicus**, and **Ramsay Hunt syndrome**.
  - **EBV (HHV-4)**: **infectious mononucleosis (glandular fever)** and associated cancers.
  - **CMV (HHV-5)**: mostly asymptomatic in the immunocompetent; **congenital CMV** and disease in **immunosuppressed** hosts (transplant, HIV).
  - **HHV-6 and HHV-7**: **roseola infantum** (exanthem subitum, a common cause of febrile convulsions in infants), reactivation after transplant. **HHV-8**: **Kaposi sarcoma**, primary effusion lymphoma and multicentric Castleman disease.

  > **Microbiology and pathology**
  >
  > - **Structure**: enveloped, icosahedral, **large linear dsDNA viruses**. Subfamilies reflect latency sites: **alpha** (HSV-1/2, VZV; **sensory neurons**), **beta** (CMV, HHV-6/7; **monocytes, myeloid precursors, lymphocytes**), **gamma** (EBV, HHV-8; **B lymphocytes**).
  > - **Latency**: the viral genome persists as an episome with minimal gene expression, escaping immune surveillance; **reactivation** follows loss of T-cell control (stress, UV, immunosuppression, HIV, ageing). **Cell-mediated immunity, especially CD8⁺ T cells and NK cells, controls infection**, so T-cell deficiency produces severe herpesvirus disease.
  > - **Immune evasion**: HSV **ICP47** blocks TAP-dependent peptide loading (reducing MHC-I display); CMV encodes multiple **US2/3/6/11 proteins that downregulate MHC-I** and **MHC-I homologues that decoy NK cells**; EBV and CMV produce **viral IL-10 homologues**.
  > - **EBV**: enters B cells via **CD21 (CR2) with gp350** and MHC-II as co-receptor; **LMP1 mimics constitutively active CD40** and LMP2A mimics B-cell receptor signalling, driving B-cell survival. The "atypical lymphocytes" of glandular fever are **activated CD8⁺ cytotoxic T cells** responding to infected B cells. **Associated malignancy**: endemic **Burkitt lymphoma** (t(8;14) *MYC* translocation, with malaria as a cofactor), **nasopharyngeal carcinoma**, Hodgkin lymphoma, **post-transplant lymphoproliferative disease** and CNS lymphoma in HIV.
  > - **Antivirals**: **aciclovir/valaciclovir/famciclovir** are phosphorylated by **viral thymidine kinase** (so are selective) and terminate DNA chain elongation via viral DNA polymerase. **CMV** lacks the same TK (it uses UL97 kinase), so **ganciclovir** is used; **foscarnet** (pyrophosphate analogue) and **cidofovir** act independently of viral kinases and are used for resistant strains.
diagnosis: |+
  HSV

  - **Orolabial herpes** (tingling then vesicles); **genital herpes**: painful vesicles/ulcers, dysuria, lymphadenopathy, systemic upset (**primary** episodes are worse than recurrences, which are shorter and milder).
  - **HSV encephalitis**: fever, confusion, behaviour change, seizures, focal signs (see the CNS infections card) — a medical emergency. **Neonatal herpes**: skin/eye/mouth disease, CNS disease or disseminated disease.
  - **Eczema herpeticum**: widespread vesicular, punched-out erosions in atopic skin with fever — treat urgently.

  VZV

  - **Chickenpox**: itchy vesicular rash in crops, fever; **adults, pregnant women and the immunosuppressed** can develop pneumonitis, encephalitis, or severe disseminated disease. **Shingles**: dermatomal pain then unilateral vesicular rash; **postherpetic neuralgia** is the main late complication in older patients.
  - **Herpes zoster ophthalmicus** (V1 distribution; **Hutchinson's sign** — vesicles on the tip of the nose, indicating nasociliary nerve involvement) threatens vision. **Ramsay Hunt syndrome**: zoster of the geniculate ganglion with **ipsilateral facial palsy, ear pain and vesicles in the ear canal/pinna**.

  EBV, CMV and HHV-6/8

  - **Glandular fever**: fever, **exudative pharyngitis**, **posterior cervical lymphadenopathy**, marked fatigue, splenomegaly, and sometimes hepatitis; **amoxicillin/ampicillin causes a diffuse rash**.
  - **CMV in the immunosuppressed**: **retinitis** (CD4 <50 in HIV), colitis, pneumonitis, hepatitis, and CMV syndrome after transplant (donor-positive, recipient-negative = highest risk). **Congenital CMV**: the commonest non-genetic cause of **sensorineural hearing loss**, with periventricular calcification, microcephaly and chorioretinitis.
  - **HHV-6**: high fever for 3–5 days in an infant, then a rash as the fever settles. **HHV-8**: violaceous skin/mucosal lesions of Kaposi sarcoma, especially in HIV.
investigations: |+
  - **HSV/VZV**: **PCR of vesicle swab** (preferred), CSF PCR for encephalitis/meningitis, and conjunctival/corneal samples in eye disease. HSV serology is **not** used to diagnose acute disease.
  - **EBV**: **FBC and film** (lymphocytosis with atypical lymphocytes), **heterophile antibody (Monospot)** (can be negative in the first week; repeat or send EBV-specific serology — VCA IgM, EBNA), LFTs.
  - **CMV**: **PCR for viral load** (blood), tissue histology (owl's-eye intranuclear inclusions), **fundoscopy** in suspected retinitis, and **pp65 antigenaemia** where used. **Congenital CMV**: urine/saliva PCR within the first 3 weeks of life.
  - **HIV test** for any severe, atypical or recurrent herpesvirus disease.
treatment: |+
  - **Genital herpes (BASHH)**: **oral aciclovir 400 mg three times daily for 5 days** (or valaciclovir/famciclovir alternatives), started within 5 days of onset or while new lesions form; **suppressive therapy** for frequent recurrences (≥6 a year). **Pregnancy**: first-episode genital herpes in the third trimester and active lesions at delivery are reasons to discuss **caesarean section** and aciclovir cover with obstetrics/infectious diseases.
  - **HSV encephalitis, neonatal herpes, severe/disseminated disease or immunosuppression**: **IV aciclovir** (10 mg/kg every 8 hours in adult encephalitis; renal dose adjustment and hydration). **Aciclovir-resistant HSV** (thymidine kinase mutants, in the immunosuppressed): **foscarnet**.
  - **Shingles**: **oral aciclovir 800 mg five times daily for 7 days** or **valaciclovir 1 g three times daily**, ideally within 72 hours of rash (still worthwhile later in higher-risk or complicated disease); analgesia, and treat **zoster ophthalmicus** with urgent ophthalmology review. **Chickenpox**: aciclovir in adults, the immunosuppressed or pregnant women within 24 hours of rash; **varicella zoster immunoglobulin (VZIG)** for **susceptible, exposed pregnant, immunosuppressed and newborn contacts** on UKHSA advice. **Recombinant adjuvanted zoster vaccine (Shingrix)** is used in the UK programme for older adults and eligible immunocompromised groups. Chickenpox vaccination is also available for selected groups.
  - **Glandular fever**: supportive; **avoid contact sports for about 4 weeks** (splenic rupture risk); steroids only for airway obstruction or severe complications.
  - **CMV**: **ganciclovir IV (or oral valganciclovir)** for disease in immunosuppressed patients, with prophylaxis/pre-emptive monitoring in transplant recipients (**letermovir** is used for prophylaxis); monitor for **neutropenia** (ganciclovir) and **nephrotoxicity** (foscarnet/cidofovir). Congenital CMV with symptoms: antiviral therapy under specialist guidance.
protocols: |+
  > Red flag: **suspected HSV encephalitis** — start **IV aciclovir immediately**, before CT, LP or MRI results. Delay of even a day worsens outcome.

  - **Neonatal herpes**, **eczema herpeticum**, **herpes zoster ophthalmicus** and disseminated VZV/HSV in the immunosuppressed are all emergencies needing urgent antivirals and specialist input.
  - **Chickenpox in pregnancy**: seek urgent advice; early treatment and VZIG if exposed and susceptible (**fetal varicella syndrome** risk in the first 20 weeks; neonatal varicella if maternal rash occurs near delivery).
  - **Infection control**: chickenpox and disseminated zoster require **airborne precautions**; staff without immunity should not care for these patients. Check the varicella immune status of pregnant or immunosuppressed contacts.
  - Severe EBV airway obstruction (massive tonsillar swelling), **splenic rupture** (sudden left upper quadrant/shoulder-tip pain) or haemolytic anaemia/thrombocytopenia need urgent assessment.
