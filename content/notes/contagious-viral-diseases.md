background: |+
  Highly transmissible viral infections where **infection control and public health action** matter as much as treatment. Chickenpox is covered in the Herpesviruses card and norovirus in the Gastrointestinal infections card.

  COVID-19

  - **SARS-CoV-2** (an enveloped positive-sense RNA betacoronavirus). Spread mainly by respiratory droplets and aerosols. Spectrum from asymptomatic infection to severe pneumonia, ARDS and multiorgan failure. **Risk factors for severe disease**: older age, immunosuppression, obesity, diabetes, chronic cardiac/respiratory/renal disease, and being unvaccinated.

  Influenza

  - **Influenza A and B** (segmented negative-sense RNA orthomyxoviruses). Seasonal winter epidemics. Spread by droplets and fomites. The highest-risk groups are older people, pregnant women, those with chronic disease or immunosuppression, and young children.

  Measles

  - **Morbillivirus (paramyxovirus)**; **among the most contagious infections known (R₀ about 12–18)**, spread airborne, infectious from the prodrome until ~4 days after the rash appears. Outbreaks occur where MMR coverage falls below the ~95% needed for herd immunity.

  Mumps

  - **Rubulavirus (paramyxovirus)**; spread by droplets and saliva. Incubation 14–25 days. Outbreaks in young adults (waning or incomplete vaccine immunity).

  > **Microbiology and pathology**
  >
  > - **SARS-CoV-2**: the **spike protein binds ACE2** (primed by the host protease TMPRSS2) on airway, gut and endothelial cells. Disease is driven by diffuse alveolar damage, a **dysregulated interferon response** (delayed type I IFN with later exuberant IL-6, TNF-α, IL-1β), **endothelial injury and microthrombosis**, and lymphopenia. Late severe disease is largely **immunopathology** — the rationale for dexamethasone and IL-6/JAK inhibition.
  > - **Influenza**: **haemagglutinin (HA)** binds sialic acid receptors and mediates entry; **neuraminidase (NA)** releases new virions (the oseltamivir target). **Antigenic drift** (point mutations) causes yearly epidemics and vaccine updates; **antigenic shift** (reassortment of segments between strains, often animal reservoirs) can cause pandemics. Damage to ciliated epithelium and macrophage function predisposes to **secondary bacterial pneumonia** (*S. aureus*, pneumococcus).
  > - **Measles**: enters via **SLAM (CD150) on lymphocytes and dendritic cells**, then **nectin-4** on epithelium. It causes marked lymphopenia and **"immune amnesia"** (loss of pre-existing memory B/T cells) leaving patients susceptible to other infections for months to years. **SSPE** is a late, fatal persistent-infection complication.
  > - **Mumps**: the virus infects salivary gland epithelium (parotitis), then disseminates to the testes, CNS and pancreas.
diagnosis: |+
  - **COVID-19**: fever, cough, breathlessness, fatigue, myalgia, anosmia/ageusia, headache, diarrhoea. Severe disease: hypoxia (**"silent" hypoxaemia** is typical), bilateral infiltrates, thromboembolism. **Long COVID** (symptoms persisting beyond 12 weeks) is covered by NICE NG188.
  - **Influenza**: **abrupt** high fever, myalgia, headache, dry cough, malaise. **Complications**: primary viral pneumonia, secondary bacterial pneumonia, myocarditis, encephalitis, Guillain–Barré syndrome.
  - **Measles**: **prodrome** (fever, cough, coryza, conjunctivitis), **Koplik spots** (white spots on the buccal mucosa), then a **maculopapular rash starting behind the ears and face and spreading down**. **Complications**: otitis media, pneumonia (commonest cause of death), diarrhoea, encephalitis, SSPE (years later); severe in malnutrition, pregnancy and immunosuppression.
  - **Mumps**: fever, malaise and **unilateral or bilateral parotitis**; **orchitis** (post-pubertal males, usually unilateral; rarely causes infertility), aseptic meningitis, pancreatitis, oophoritis, and sensorineural deafness.
investigations: |+
  - **COVID-19**: **PCR or lateral flow antigen test** (upper respiratory sample). Bloods: lymphopenia, **raised CRP, ferritin, LDH and D-dimer** in severe disease. CXR/CT: bilateral peripheral ground-glass opacities. Check oxygen saturations and ABG if hypoxic.
  - **Influenza**: **PCR on a respiratory sample** (nose/throat swab), usually as a multiplex respiratory panel with other viruses. CXR if pneumonia suspected; consider a bacterial co-infection.
  - **Measles**: **notify on clinical suspicion**, then confirm with **oral fluid (or serum) IgM and PCR** (arranged via UKHSA). **Mumps**: oral fluid/serum IgM and PCR (also notifiable).
  - Other: FBC, U&E, LFTs, blood cultures if septic; HIV and immune status in severe or atypical disease.
treatment: |+
  COVID-19 (NICE NG191; eligibility has changed repeatedly — check current NICE TA878 criteria and local policy)

  - **Supportive care**, oxygen titration, VTE prophylaxis, and **dexamethasone 6 mg once daily for up to 10 days** for patients needing supplemental oxygen. For hypoxic patients with a significant inflammatory response, **IL-6 receptor blockade (tocilizumab) and/or baricitinib** are considered under local policy.
  - **Antivirals for high-risk patients** (for example **nirmatrelvir/ritonavir**, with a careful **drug-interaction check** because ritonavir inhibits CYP3A4; **remdesivir** or other agents in defined hospital settings) are restricted to those at highest risk of progression; **NICE narrowed the groups for nirmatrelvir/ritonavir in 2025** and sotrovimab is no longer available in the UK. **Don't use azithromycin or doxycycline for COVID-19** itself.

  Influenza (UKHSA antiviral guidance)

  - **Oseltamivir (neuraminidase inhibitor)**, ideally within 48 hours of onset, for those at risk of complications and for anyone with severe or complicated illness (treat hospitalised patients regardless of timing). Treat bacterial superinfection separately. Annual vaccination for at-risk groups and healthcare workers.

  Measles and mumps

  - **Supportive care only.** **MMR vaccination** (two doses) is the main control measure. **Post-exposure MMR (ideally within 72 hours)** or **human normal immunoglobulin (up to 6 days)** for susceptible vulnerable contacts (pregnant women, infants, immunosuppressed) on UKHSA advice. Vitamin A is used in severe measles in some settings (WHO).
  - **Notify, isolate, trace contacts.** Measles: **airborne precautions** (negative-pressure/side room); exclusion from school/work for 4 days after rash onset. Mumps: **droplet precautions**; exclusion for 5 days after onset of parotitis.
protocols: |+
  > Red flag: suspected measles or any suspected high-consequence infection in a clinic or emergency department: **don't leave the patient in the waiting room.** Isolate immediately in a side room, wear appropriate PPE (FFP3 for airborne infection), and phone infection control and the health protection team.

  - **Infection control summary**: COVID-19 and influenza — droplet/aerosol precautions, with FFP3 respirators for aerosol-generating procedures; **measles (and chickenpox) — airborne isolation**; mumps — droplet precautions. Check the immune status of staff and contacts.
  - **Notifiable diseases**: measles, mumps, rubella, and others are notifiable on **clinical suspicion** to the proper officer (local health protection team) — do not wait for laboratory confirmation. Influenza and COVID-19 outbreaks in hospitals and care homes also trigger outbreak management.
  - **Severe COVID-19 or influenza pneumonia** with hypoxia: see the Respiratory failure card (Respiratory) for oxygen/NIV/escalation decisions.
