background: |+
  "Lower respiratory tract infection" spans everything from acute bronchitis (its own card) to pneumonia — infection of the lung parenchyma itself, seen as consolidation on imaging. This card focuses on **pneumonia** and its complications; the core severity scoring and first-line antibiotic ladder are covered in the Sepsis and infection factfile (Acute medicine) and are only summarised here to avoid repetition.

  - **Community-acquired pneumonia (CAP)**: *Streptococcus pneumoniae* is the commonest cause; also *Haemophilus influenzae*, atypical organisms (*Mycoplasma*, *Chlamydophila pneumoniae*, *Legionella*), and viruses (influenza, SARS-CoV-2) — *Staphylococcus aureus* classically follows influenza infection and can cause rapidly progressive, cavitating disease.
  - **Hospital-acquired pneumonia (HAP)**: onset **>48 hours after admission** — Gram-negative organisms (*Pseudomonas*, *Klebsiella*, *E. coli*) and *S. aureus* (including MRSA) are more prominent than in CAP, reflecting a different, often healthcare-exposed flora.
  - **Aspiration pneumonia**: follows aspiration of oropharyngeal or gastric contents, in patients with an impaired swallow (stroke, reduced consciousness, oesophageal disease) or reduced cough reflex — typically affects the **right lower lobe** (more vertical, wider right main bronchus) and often involves mixed aerobic and anaerobic oral flora.
  - **Immunocompromised patients** (HIV, chemotherapy, long-term steroids/biologics) need a broader differential, including *Pneumocystis jirovecii* pneumonia (PCP) and fungal/viral causes not typically seen in the immunocompetent.
diagnosis: |+
  - Fever, productive cough, pleuritic chest pain, breathlessness; in older patients, **confusion may be the dominant or only obvious presenting feature**, with fewer typical respiratory symptoms. Examination: focal coarse crackles, bronchial breathing, dullness to percussion, increased vocal resonance/tactile fremitus over the consolidated area.
  - **Atypical pneumonia clues**: a more gradual onset, a dry cough, extrapulmonary features (headache, myalgia, diarrhoea), and a CXR that can look out of proportion to (worse than) the clinical examination. *Legionella* specifically: hyponatraemia, deranged LFTs, confusion, and a history of recent travel, air conditioning or spa/hot tub exposure.
  - **Severity assessment (CURB-65)** and the initial antibiotic ladder by severity are detailed in the Sepsis and infection factfile — use that scoring and ladder here rather than duplicating it.
investigations: |+
  - **CXR** confirms consolidation and can suggest a complication (cavitation, effusion). Repeat imaging (around 6 weeks later) is advised in smokers and older patients, or anyone with ongoing symptoms, specifically to make sure an underlying endobronchial lesion (lung cancer) isn't the real explanation for a "pneumonia" that doesn't fully resolve (see Lung cancer card).
  - Standard severity work-up (FBC, U&E, CRP, blood cultures, sputum culture, SpO₂/ABG) and specific tests for atypical organisms (urinary *Legionella* and pneumococcal antigen in moderate-severe disease, respiratory viral PCR) are detailed in the Sepsis and infection factfile.
  - **Non-resolving pneumonia** (persisting beyond the expected timeframe despite appropriate antibiotics): reconsider the diagnosis and look for complications — repeat/extend imaging (CT chest), bronchoscopy if an endobronchial obstruction (tumour, foreign body) is suspected, and reassess for resistant or unusual organisms (including TB — see that card), empyema, or a non-infective mimic (organising pneumonia, vasculitis, pulmonary oedema).
  - **Suspected lung abscess**: CT chest showing a cavitating lesion, often with an air-fluid level.
treatment: |+
  - Antibiotic choice by severity (CAP) and the general approach to sepsis is covered in the Sepsis and infection factfile — use that ladder. This card adds detail for specific scenarios not covered there:
  - **Aspiration pneumonia**: antibiotic choice should cover anaerobes in addition to the usual CAP/HAP organisms (e.g. co-amoxiclav, or metronidazole added to a standard regimen) — follow local policy. Address the underlying cause of aspiration (swallow assessment by speech and language therapy, positioning, treating the precipitant) to prevent recurrence, which matters as much as the antibiotic course itself.
  - **Hospital-acquired pneumonia**: broader-spectrum antibiotics reflecting the different likely organisms (per local policy, often covering *Pseudomonas*) — narrower than for suspected MRSA/resistant organisms only where risk factors are present.
  - **Lung abscess**: prolonged antibiotic course (often several weeks), guided by culture where possible; most resolve with antibiotics and postural drainage alone; persistent/large abscesses or failure to improve may need image-guided or surgical drainage.
  - **Complicated parapneumonic effusion/empyema**: needs chest drain and, often, intrapleural fibrinolytics or surgery in addition to antibiotics — full detail (pleural fluid pH/glucose thresholds, drain technique) is in the Pleural disease factfile (Acute medicine); this is one of the commonest reasons a "straightforward" pneumonia fails to settle.
  - **Pneumocystis jirovecii pneumonia (PCP)**: co-trimoxazole (high dose) is first-line, with adjunctive corticosteroids if significantly hypoxic (PaO₂ <9.3 kPa on room air) — managed with HIV/infectious diseases input.
protocols: |+
  - **Sepsis Six** and the CURB-65-based escalation pathway for pneumonia are detailed in the Sepsis and infection factfile — apply that pathway here; this card does not duplicate it.
  - **Suspected empyema or lung abscess with systemic deterioration** needs the same urgency as any undrained collection of infected material — source control (drainage) is as important as antibiotics, and delay worsens outcome.
  - A pneumonia that is **not improving as expected, or recurs repeatedly in the same place**, is a red flag for an underlying endobronchial obstruction (most importantly lung cancer) — this should trigger further imaging/referral rather than simply repeating or extending antibiotics indefinitely.
