background: |+
  Infections acquired through healthcare contact, usually developing **48 hours or more after admission** (or within days of a procedure or discharge). They occur because of **vulnerable patients, invasive devices and antibiotic pressure** (selecting resistant organisms and disrupting the normal flora).

  - ***Clostridioides difficile* infection (CDI)**: antibiotic-associated diarrhoea and colitis. **Risk factors**: recent broad-spectrum antibiotics (**clindamycin, cephalosporins, fluoroquinolones, co-amoxiclav**), age >65, hospital or care-home stay, PPIs, immunosuppression, and previous CDI.
  - **Hospital-acquired pneumonia (HAP)**: onset **≥48 hours after admission**; **ventilator-associated pneumonia (VAP)** after intubation. Organisms include Gram-negative bacilli (*Pseudomonas*, *Klebsiella*, *E. coli*), *S. aureus* (including MRSA) and anaerobes (aspiration).
  - **MRSA**: meticillin-resistant *S. aureus* causing skin/soft tissue, wound, bone and bloodstream infection.
  - **Device-associated**: **catheter-associated UTI**, **central line-associated bloodstream infection (CLABSI)** (coagulase-negative staphylococci, *S. aureus*, Candida), ventilator pneumonia, and **surgical site infection**.
  - **Multidrug-resistant Gram-negatives**: **ESBL**-producing Enterobacterales and **carbapenemase-producing organisms (CPE)**; *Pseudomonas aeruginosa*. Vancomycin-resistant enterococci (VRE).

  > **Microbiology and pathology**
  >
  > - ***C. difficile***: Gram-positive, anaerobic, **spore-forming bacillus**. Loss of colonisation resistance (a diverse microbiota with secondary bile acid metabolism) lets spores germinate. It produces **toxin A (TcdA, enterotoxin) and toxin B (TcdB, cytotoxin)**, which **glucosylate Rho/Rac GTPases**, disrupting the actin cytoskeleton, tight junctions and epithelial survival; the host response (neutrophils, IL-8) produces **pseudomembranes** (fibrin, mucus, necrotic epithelium and neutrophils). **Hypervirulent ribotype 027** also produces binary toxin and overproduces toxins. **Spores resist alcohol gel and persist on surfaces**, so soap-and-water handwashing and sporicidal (chlorine-based) cleaning are essential.
  > - **Antibiotic resistance mechanisms**: **MRSA** carries **mecA** (on the SCCmec element) encoding **PBP2a**, which has low affinity for β-lactams. **ESBLs** hydrolyse penicillins and cephalosporins but not carbapenems. **Carbapenemases (KPC, NDM, OXA-48, VIM)** inactivate carbapenems. Resistance genes spread on **plasmids and transposons** (horizontal gene transfer), not just by mutation.
  > - **Biofilm**: catheters and lines are colonised by organisms that embed in an extracellular matrix, becoming tolerant to antibiotics and immune clearance — hence the need to **remove or replace the device** for most device infections.
diagnosis: |+
  - **CDI**: **≥3 loose stools (Bristol type 5–7) in 24 hours** with no other explanation, often with abdominal pain, fever, leucocytosis and (in severe disease) abdominal distension. **Severity (NICE NG199)**: **severe** if any of **WCC >15 × 10⁹/L, creatinine >50% above baseline, temperature >38.5 °C**, or severe colitis on imaging/clinical signs. **Life-threatening** disease: hypotension, ileus, toxic megacolon, or CT evidence of severe colitis.
  - **HAP**: new infiltrate with fever, purulent sputum, hypoxia or deterioration ≥48 hours after admission. Always consider **aspiration**.
  - **CAUTI**: fever, suprapubic/loin pain, new delirium or rigors in a catheterised patient (**bacteriuria alone is not infection**); **CLABSI**: fever or rigors, especially with line use, and erythema/pus at the line site.
  - **MRSA**: skin abscess, wound infection, or bacteraemia (with bone/joint/endocarditis foci).
investigations: |+
  - **CDI**: **stool testing** for toxin/glutamate dehydrogenase (GDH) with confirmatory PCR per a two-step algorithm. **Test only patients with unexplained diarrhoea** (don't test formed stool). FBC, U&E, CRP, lactate; **imaging** for severe disease (distension, megacolon, perforation).
  - **HAP**: CXR/CT, FBC, CRP, U&E, blood cultures, **sputum or endotracheal aspirate culture** (before changing antibiotics where possible), and MRSA screen results.
  - **Catheter/line infection**: **paired blood cultures from the line and a peripheral site**; catheter urine culture; consider line tip culture after removal.
  - **MRSA/CPE screening** on admission for defined risk groups per local policy; **decolonisation** (chlorhexidine body wash, nasal mupirocin) for MRSA carriers undergoing certain procedures.
treatment: |+
  Clostridioides difficile infection (NICE NG199)

  - **Stop or review the precipitating antibiotic** and PPI where possible. Maintain hydration and electrolytes; **avoid antimotility agents**.
  - **First-line: oral vancomycin 125 mg four times daily for 10 days.** **Second-line: fidaxomicin 200 mg twice daily for 10 days**, and for **recurrence within 12 weeks** of symptom resolution. Recurrence after 12 weeks: oral vancomycin or fidaxomicin. **Life-threatening CDI**: oral vancomycin (up to 500 mg four times daily) **plus IV metronidazole 500 mg three times daily**, with urgent surgical and ITU review. **Colectomy** is considered for toxic megacolon, perforation or refractory shock. **Faecal microbiota transplantation (FMT)** is used for multiple recurrences under specialist guidance. Don't use antibiotic prophylaxis or probiotics to prevent CDI.

  Hospital-acquired pneumonia (NICE NG139)

  - Choose the antibiotic by **risk of resistance, severity, local resistance patterns and previous cultures**, review after culture results, and keep the course short (typically **5 days**, reassess at 48–72 hours). **Co-amoxiclav** is first-line for lower-risk HAP; those who are severely unwell or at higher risk of resistant organisms need **broader cover** (including an antipseudomonal agent ± MRSA cover) under local policy. Treat aspiration pneumonia with anaerobic cover and address swallowing (see the LRTI card, Respiratory).

  Device-associated and resistant infections

  - **CAUTI (NICE NG113)**: treat only if symptomatic; **remove or replace the catheter** (if in place >7 days). **CLABSI**: remove the line where possible; *S. aureus* bacteraemia needs **≥14 days of IV therapy**, echocardiography and infectious diseases input.
  - **MRSA infection**: **vancomycin or teicoplanin** (IV); linezolid, doxycycline or clindamycin as alternatives for milder or oral therapy by sensitivities. **ESBL** infection: a **carbapenem** (or an active alternative by sensitivities); CPE infection needs specialist microbiology input.
  - **Prevention**: **hand hygiene** (WHO "5 moments"), aseptic technique, "bare below the elbows", isolation/cohorting, environmental cleaning, remove unnecessary devices early, and **antimicrobial stewardship ("Start smart, then focus")**.
protocols: |+
  > Red flag: **severe or life-threatening *C. difficile*** (distension, absent bowel sounds, hypotension, rising lactate, WCC >15) needs same-day surgical and critical care involvement — delay in colectomy for toxic megacolon increases mortality.

  - **Infection control**: patients with CDI, MRSA, CPE or other transmissible organisms need **side-room isolation, contact precautions, soap-and-water hand hygiene (for CDI), and dedicated equipment**. Report **outbreaks** and bacteraemia (MRSA, *S. aureus*, *C. difficile*, Gram-negative) as required under mandatory surveillance.
  - **Sepsis** from any hospital-acquired infection: follow the Sepsis Six pathway (see the Sepsis and infection factfile, Acute medicine).
  - **Recurrent CDI or failure to respond**: refer to infectious diseases/gastroenterology and consider alternative or concurrent diagnoses (CMV colitis, IBD flare).
