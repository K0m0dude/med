background: |+
  Infections of the epidermis, dermis, subcutaneous tissue and fascia. Most are straightforward and treated in the community; a small number are **fulminant, limb- or life-threatening**.

  - **Impetigo**: superficial; **non-bullous** (*S. aureus* ± group A strep) or **bullous** (*S. aureus*). Highly contagious, common in children.
  - **Folliculitis, furuncles (boils), carbuncles and abscesses**: *S. aureus*, including **PVL-producing** strains (recurrent boils, necrotising pneumonia) and MRSA.
  - **Cellulitis** (deeper dermis and subcutaneous fat) and **erysipelas** (more superficial, sharply demarcated, raised edge, lymphatic involvement): **group A streptococci and *S. aureus***. Predisposing factors: skin barrier breach (tinea pedis, eczema, ulcers, wounds), **lymphoedema, venous insufficiency, obesity, diabetes**, previous cellulitis, IV drug use.
  - **Necrotising soft tissue infection**: **type I** (polymicrobial: anaerobes and Enterobacterales, often diabetics, perineum/Fournier's gangrene), **type II** (group A streptococcus ± *S. aureus*, including in previously healthy people), **type III** (*Clostridium* — gas gangrene; marine *Vibrio*).
  - **Toxin-mediated disease**: **staphylococcal scalded skin syndrome** (exfoliative toxins), **toxic shock syndrome** (staphylococcal TSST-1 or streptococcal pyrogenic exotoxins).
  - Also **bite wounds** (*Pasteurella*, *Capnocytophaga*, anaerobes) and diabetic foot infection (see the diabetes complications notes).

  > **Microbiology and pathology**
  >
  > - ***S. aureus*** produces **coagulase, protein A (binds IgG Fc, blocking opsonisation)**, **α-toxin**, and **Panton–Valentine leukocidin (PVL)**, which lyses neutrophils. **Exfoliative toxins A/B** are serine proteases that **cleave desmoglein-1** in the stratum granulosum, producing blistering (bullous impetigo, scalded skin syndrome).
  > - ***S. pyogenes***: **M protein** (antiphagocytic), hyaluronic acid capsule, **streptolysins**, **streptokinase and hyaluronidase** (tissue spread), and **SpeB protease**. These explain the rapid spread in erysipelas and necrotising fasciitis.
  > - **Superantigens** (TSST-1, streptococcal pyrogenic exotoxins A/C) **bridge MHC class II and the T-cell receptor Vβ chain outside the peptide-binding groove**, activating up to ~20% of T cells polyclonally, causing a massive **IL-2, TNF-α and IFN-γ** release → fever, rash, hypotension and multi-organ failure.
  > - **Necrotising fasciitis**: infection spreads along the fascial planes, causing vascular thrombosis, **tissue ischaemia and necrosis**; nerves are damaged early (hence **pain out of proportion**, then numbness). *Clostridium perfringens* **α-toxin (a phospholipase/lecithinase)** lyses cell membranes and causes gas gangrene.
diagnosis: |+
  - **Cellulitis/erysipelas**: **spreading erythema, warmth, swelling and pain**, often with fever; **mark the border** and review. The **commonest diagnostic error is a mimic**: **venous (stasis) eczema, lipodermatosclerosis, contact dermatitis, gout, a DVT**, and lymphoedema without infection. Bilateral "cellulitis" is rarely infective.
  - **Severity**: assess systemic features (NEWS2, sepsis screen), the rate of spread, comorbidity and the patient's frailty.
  - **Impetigo**: honey-coloured crusts (non-bullous) or flaccid bullae (bullous). **Abscess**: a fluctuant, tender collection.
  - **Necrotising infection — diagnosis is clinical and urgent**: **pain out of proportion to the findings**, rapid spread, **dusky or violaceous skin, bullae, anaesthesia over the area, crepitus**, severe systemic toxicity, and failure to respond to antibiotics. **A normal-looking skin does not exclude deep necrotising infection.**
  - **Toxic shock syndrome**: fever, **diffuse erythroderma ("sunburn" rash)**, hypotension, multi-organ involvement, and later desquamation (palms and soles); look for a focus (tampon, wound, post-operative site, soft tissue infection).
investigations: |+
  - **Cellulitis**: clinical diagnosis. **Bloods** (CRP, WCC, U&E, glucose) for more severe disease; **blood cultures if systemic features**; **swab/aspirate pus** from abscesses or wounds; **ultrasound** to confirm or localise an abscess; image or examine for DVT or deeper infection when unclear.
  - **Necrotising fasciitis**: the **LRINEC score** (CRP, WCC, Hb, sodium, creatinine, glucose) may support suspicion, but **its sensitivity is poor and a low score must not delay surgery**. **Surgical exploration is diagnostic**; CT/MRI can help if the patient is stable but must not delay surgical review.
  - **Recurrent boils/PVL**: **screen** the patient and household contacts for *S. aureus* carriage and PVL genes, and decolonise.
  - **Lactate, blood gas, CK and clotting** in severe infection.
treatment: |+
  Cellulitis and erysipelas (NICE NG141)

  - **First-line oral antibiotic: flucloxacillin for 5–7 days** (500 mg–1 g four times daily); **if penicillin-allergic**: **clarithromycin** (**erythromycin in pregnancy**) or **doxycycline**. Reassess at 2–3 days; extend to up to 14 days in slow responders. **IV therapy** for systemic features, rapidly progressive infection or failure of oral treatment (for example IV flucloxacillin; **IV co-amoxiclav, cefuroxime or clindamycin** by local policy; **vancomycin or teicoplanin** for suspected MRSA).
  - Elevate the limb, treat tinea and oedema, use **compression** (after infection is controlled) for venous disease/lymphoedema, and give analgesia. **Don't routinely prescribe antibiotics to prevent recurrence** (NG141); manage predisposing factors.

  Impetigo (NICE NG153)

  - **Localised**: **topical hydrogen peroxide 1% cream** (first-line, 5 days) (**topical fusidic acid is not first-line** because of resistance); topical antibiotic alternatives per guidance. **Widespread, severe or recurrent** impetigo: **oral flucloxacillin** (clarithromycin or erythromycin if penicillin-allergic). Reduce spread: hygiene, school exclusion until lesions are crusted or 48 hours after antibiotics.

  Abscesses, necrotising infection and toxic shock

  - **Abscess: incision and drainage** is the definitive treatment; antibiotics as an adjunct only for systemic features, extensive cellulitis, high-risk patients or MRSA.
  - **Necrotising fasciitis: emergency surgical debridement (repeated as needed) plus IV broad-spectrum antibiotics** (for example **piperacillin–tazobactam or a carbapenem, plus clindamycin**, which suppresses toxin production, ± vancomycin for MRSA), critical care support, and sometimes **IV immunoglobulin** for streptococcal toxic shock. Don't delay for imaging or "wait and see".
  - **Toxic shock syndrome**: **source control** (remove the tampon/foreign body, drain/debride), **IV antibiotics (flucloxacillin or equivalent plus clindamycin)**, fluids and organ support; IV immunoglobulin in selected cases.
  - **MRSA/PVL**: decolonisation (chlorhexidine, nasal mupirocin), hygiene measures and antibiotics per sensitivities.
protocols: |+
  > Red flag: **pain out of proportion to the skin appearance, rapidly spreading erythema, bullae, skin necrosis, crepitus or systemic collapse** = **necrotising fasciitis until proven otherwise**. This is a surgical emergency — call the surgical team and critical care immediately, and don't delay for imaging.

  - **Periorbital vs orbital cellulitis**: **periorbital** (eyelid swelling and erythema only) usually responds to antibiotics. **Orbital cellulitis** (**pain on eye movement, restricted movement/ophthalmoplegia, proptosis, reduced visual acuity or colour vision, or relative afferent pupillary defect**) is an emergency: urgent contrast CT, IV antibiotics, ophthalmology/ENT, and watch for abscess or **cavernous sinus thrombosis**.
  - **Facial cellulitis around the nose/eyes**: risk of intracranial spread (cavernous sinus thrombosis; see the CNS infections card).
  - **Sepsis** from soft tissue infection: follow the Sepsis Six pathway (see the Sepsis and infection factfile, Acute medicine). **Cellulitis not responding after 48 hours**: re-examine for abscess, necrotising infection, DVT, or a mimic.
