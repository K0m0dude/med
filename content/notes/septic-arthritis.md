background: |+
  Infection of a joint, usually bacterial. **It is an orthopaedic-medical emergency**: destruction of cartilage can occur within **days (even hours)**, so a **hot, swollen, painful joint is septic arthritis until proven otherwise**.

  - **Routes**: haematogenous spread (the commonest), direct inoculation (injection, arthroscopy, surgery, trauma, bites), and spread from adjacent osteomyelitis.
  - **Organisms**: ***Staphylococcus aureus*** (most common, including MRSA); streptococci (group A, pneumococcus); **Gram-negative bacilli** (older adults, IV drug users, immunosuppression); ***Neisseria gonorrhoeae*** (young sexually active adults, often polyarticular/migratory with tenosynovitis and pustules); *Salmonella* (sickle cell disease); *Kingella kingae* (young children); *Pasteurella* (bites); *Borrelia* (Lyme arthritis); TB and *Brucella* (subacute, monoarticular).
  - **Risk factors**: **pre-existing joint disease (especially rheumatoid arthritis)**, **joint prostheses**, diabetes, immunosuppression (steroids, biologics), older age, IV drug use, skin infection or ulcers, recent joint surgery or injection.
  - **Prosthetic joint infection (PJI)**: **early (<3 months)**, **delayed (3–24 months)** or **late (>2 years)**; coagulase-negative staphylococci and *S. aureus* predominate; biofilm on the implant is the problem.

  > **Microbiology and pathology**
  >
  > - The **synovium is highly vascular and lacks a basement membrane**, so blood-borne bacteria seed it easily. *S. aureus* binds synovial matrix proteins through adhesins (fibronectin-, collagen-binding) and produces toxins.
  > - **Cartilage destruction is largely a host response**: IL-1, TNF-α and IL-6 stimulate chondrocytes and synovial cells to release **matrix metalloproteinases**, and neutrophil proteases and oxidants add to the damage; bacterial products add direct injury. Pus under pressure also compromises the joint's blood supply.
  > - **Synovial fluid** in septic arthritis: markedly raised neutrophil count, low glucose, and often positive Gram stain/culture; crystals can **coexist** with infection.
  > - **Gonococcal arthritis** is a disseminated gonococcal infection (DGI): immune-complex-mediated tenosynovitis/dermatitis (arthritis–dermatitis syndrome) alongside, or distinct from, true septic arthritis. **Complement deficiency (terminal) predisposes to recurrent disseminated Neisseria infection.**
  > - **PJI biofilm**: bacteria embedded in an extracellular matrix on metal/polyethylene are **tolerant of antibiotics and immune cells**, so cure usually needs **implant revision or removal**, or debridement with an anti-biofilm agent such as **rifampicin** (always combined with a second agent).
diagnosis: |+
  - **Acute monoarthritis** (**knee** most commonly; hip, shoulder, wrist, ankle follow): **pain, swelling, warmth, limited movement, and pain on passive movement**, often with fever. **Fever may be absent** in older adults, immunosuppressed patients and those with prostheses — don't be reassured by a normal temperature.
  - **Children**: refusal to bear weight, limp or irritability; **hip** infection is especially destructive. **Kocher criteria** help in the irritable hip: non-weight-bearing, fever >38.5 °C, ESR >40 mm/h, WCC >12 × 10⁹/L (the more present, the higher the probability).
  - **Gonococcal**: young adult, **migratory polyarthralgia**, tenosynovitis, vesiculopustular skin lesions, ± genitourinary symptoms.
  - **Differential**: **gout/pseudogout** (can coexist), reactive arthritis, haemarthrosis, Lyme and viral arthritis, rheumatoid or other inflammatory flare, and osteomyelitis.
investigations: |+
  - **Joint aspiration (arthrocentesis) is the key investigation; take it BEFORE antibiotics** if the patient is stable, ideally under ultrasound for deep joints. Send **Gram stain, culture (including enrichment), cell count and differential, crystals (polarised light microscopy)** and, where relevant, TB, gonococcal NAAT or PCR.
  - **Synovial WCC**: usually **>50,000/mm³** in septic arthritis, but lower counts occur (including early disease and PJI), and high counts are also seen in inflammatory arthritis, so **interpret with the clinical picture**. A negative Gram stain or culture does not exclude infection.
  - **Blood cultures** (positive in a substantial proportion), FBC, CRP/ESR, U&E, LFTs, glucose. **Swabs from throat, urethra/cervix, rectum** for gonorrhoea when suspected.
  - **Imaging**: **plain X-ray** (baseline; excludes fracture, chondrocalcinosis, and shows prosthetic loosening); **ultrasound** (effusion; guides aspiration); **MRI** if osteomyelitis or a deep collection is suspected.
treatment: |+
  - **Urgent orthopaedic and microbiology/infectious diseases involvement.** **Joint drainage and washout** (arthroscopic or open; repeated aspiration in selected cases) **combined with IV antibiotics**.
  - **Empirical antibiotics after cultures**: **IV flucloxacillin 2 g four times daily**; **vancomycin or teicoplanin** if MRSA risk or penicillin allergy; add Gram-negative cover (for example ceftriaxone, ± gentamicin) in at-risk patients (older, immunosuppressed, IV drug use). **Gonococcal arthritis**: **IV ceftriaxone** (with BASHH-guided co-treatment and partner notification). Tailor to culture and sensitivities.
  - **Duration**: typically **IV treatment for about 2 weeks, then oral therapy to complete 4–6 weeks in total** (follow local policy; some regimens differ by organism and joint).
  - **Prosthetic joint infection**: **orthopaedic infection MDT**. Options: **DAIR (debridement, antibiotics and implant retention)** for early infection with a stable implant, **one- or two-stage revision** for established/late infection; prolonged antibiotics, often with rifampicin as part of combination therapy.
  - **Rehabilitation**: early physiotherapy once settled, analgesia, and treatment of predisposing factors.
protocols: |+
  > Red flag: **do not give intra-articular steroid or wait for the aspirate "to be convenient"** in a hot swollen joint. If septic arthritis is suspected, **aspirate urgently, start antibiotics and call orthopaedics the same day**. In a **prosthetic joint**, urgent specialist orthopaedic advice is essential before any aspiration.

  - **Sepsis** from septic arthritis (fever, hypotension, tachycardia): follow the Sepsis Six pathway (see the Sepsis and infection factfile, Acute medicine).
  - **Septic arthritis in a child** (especially the hip) is a surgical emergency; early drainage reduces the risk of avascular necrosis and growth disturbance.
  - **Failure to improve** on treatment: reconsider undrained collections, adjacent osteomyelitis, resistant organisms, crystal arthropathy, or a non-infective cause.
