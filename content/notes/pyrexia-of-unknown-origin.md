background: |+
  **Classical PUO** (the Petersdorf and Beeson definition, since modified): **temperature >38.3 °C on several occasions, lasting more than 3 weeks, without a diagnosis after an appropriate (outpatient or inpatient) investigation**. Variant definitions apply in particular settings:

  - **Nosocomial**: fever in a hospital inpatient (>48 hours after admission) with no infection incubating at admission, not explained after 3 days of investigation.
  - **Neutropenic**: neutrophils <0.5 × 10⁹/L, fever not explained after 3 days.
  - **HIV-associated**: HIV-positive patient with fever for more than 3 weeks outpatient (or 3 days inpatient) without a diagnosis.

  **Causes** (the commonest groups; the order varies by region and age):

  - **Infection**: **TB (especially extrapulmonary and miliary)**, endocarditis (culture-negative: HACEK, *Bartonella*, *Coxiella*), abscesses (intra-abdominal, pelvic, dental, liver), osteomyelitis/discitis, EBV and CMV, HIV, brucellosis, Q fever, malaria, visceral leishmaniasis, and others by exposure.
  - **Malignancy**: lymphoma, leukaemia, **renal cell carcinoma**, hepatocellular carcinoma, atrial myxoma, metastases.
  - **Non-infectious inflammatory disease**: **giant cell arteritis and polymyalgia rheumatica (older adults)**, **adult-onset Still's disease**, SLE, systemic vasculitis, sarcoidosis, IBD, Behçet's disease, and **autoinflammatory syndromes** (for example familial Mediterranean fever).
  - **Drug fever**, **thromboembolic disease** (PE/DVT), thyroiditis, factitious fever, and others. **A substantial minority (about a third) remain undiagnosed**, and many of these resolve spontaneously with good outcomes.

  > **Microbiology and pathology**
  >
  > - **Fever is cytokine-driven**: **IL-1, IL-6, TNF-α and interferons** act on the hypothalamus via **prostaglandin E₂** to raise the set-point. Therefore infections, malignancy and autoimmune/autoinflammatory disease can all cause an indistinguishable fever.
  > - **Autoinflammatory diseases** (for example **FMF**, *MEFV* gene/pyrin; cryopyrin-associated periodic syndromes, *NLRP3*) cause **inflammasome-driven IL-1β overproduction** without infection or autoantibodies; they respond to **colchicine** (FMF) or **IL-1 blockade (anakinra)**.
  > - **Adult-onset Still's disease**: fever spikes with salmon-pink rash and arthritis, **very high ferritin** (often with a low glycosylated fraction), neutrophilia and raised IL-18; a diagnosis of exclusion.
  > - **Lymphoma** can cause fever through tumour-derived cytokines (the **Pel–Ebstein pattern** in Hodgkin lymphoma is rare). **Culture-negative endocarditis** reflects fastidious or intracellular organisms (*Coxiella*, *Bartonella*, *Tropheryma*) or prior antibiotic use.
diagnosis: |+
  - **The first step is to confirm the fever is real and prolonged** (a reliable temperature chart; exclude factitious fever and thermoregulatory causes), then **repeat a careful history and examination**. A great many diagnoses are made by re-examining the patient.
  - **History**: travel and exposures (animals, unpasteurised milk, ticks, occupation, contacts with TB), drugs (including over-the-counter and recent changes), sexual history and HIV risk, family history of periodic fevers, previous surgery or implants, weight loss and night sweats, and any localising symptoms (headache, jaw claudication, joint pain, rash, cough, back pain).
  - **Examine**: eyes (uveitis, conjunctival petechiae), mouth/teeth, skin and nails (splinters, rash, nodules), lymph nodes, **temporal arteries**, thyroid, heart (new murmur), abdomen (hepatosplenomegaly, masses), joints, spine tenderness, rectal/prostate and pelvic examination, and recheck every few days.
  - **Red flags that should accelerate the work-up**: neurological signs, new murmur, immunosuppression, weight loss or night sweats, abnormal blood counts, and rapid deterioration.
investigations: |+
  - **Stepwise, hypothesis-driven investigation avoids the "shotgun" approach.**
  - **First-line**: FBC with film, **ESR/CRP** (a very high ESR suggests GCA, vasculitis, endocarditis, abscess or lymphoma), U&E, LFTs, **LDH**, **CK**, **ferritin** (very high in adult-onset Still's disease and haemophagocytic lymphohistiocytosis), TFTs, **three sets of blood cultures** (before antibiotics; ask the lab to hold them longer if fastidious organisms are suspected), urine culture, CXR, **HIV test**, EBV/CMV/hepatitis serology, **IGRA/Mantoux**, ANA/RF/ANCA/complement, malaria films if relevant travel, and **echocardiography** (TTE ± TOE).
  - **Imaging**: **CT chest, abdomen and pelvis**; **FDG-PET/CT** is a high-yield test in PUO (large-vessel vasculitis, occult malignancy, lymphoma, infection and inflammation) and increasingly used early.
  - **Tissue and further tests as directed**: **temporal artery biopsy** (age >50, raised ESR), **bone marrow** aspirate and biopsy, **lymph node or liver biopsy**, lumbar puncture, specific serology (Brucella, Q fever, Bartonella), and genetic testing if autoinflammatory disease is suspected.
treatment: |+
  - **Treat the cause once found.** Where no cause is found, **observe**: many cases settle spontaneously, and the prognosis is generally good.
  - **Avoid blind therapy that masks the diagnosis**:
    - **Empirical broad-spectrum antibiotics** can render cultures negative and delay the diagnosis (except when there is neutropenia or the patient is unstable).
    - **Empirical corticosteroids** can mask lymphoma, TB or vasculitis; use only after serious causes are excluded or in **suspected GCA** (where delay risks irreversible blindness).
  - **Empirical trials** (selected cases, specialist guided): **antituberculous therapy** for strongly suspected TB, or **NSAIDs/corticosteroids/IL-1 blockers** for suspected inflammatory disease after infection and malignancy are excluded.
  - **Supportive**: antipyretics for symptom relief, hydration, and nutrition. Review all drugs for **drug fever** and stop the likely culprit.
protocols: |+
  > Red flag: a patient with **fever and neutropenia** (neutrophils <0.5 × 10⁹/L) is **not** a PUO — treat as neutropenic sepsis immediately with IV broad-spectrum antibiotics (see the Cancer and oncology emergencies factfile, Acute medicine).

  - **Suspected giant cell arteritis** (age >50, new headache, jaw claudication, visual symptoms, raised ESR/CRP): start **high-dose prednisolone immediately** (do not wait for biopsy) and arrange urgent review to protect vision.
  - **Suspected endocarditis, spinal infection or intracranial infection**: treat as urgent diagnoses rather than waiting out the PUO timeframe.
  - **Immunocompromised patients and travellers** often need an accelerated, broader work-up (including opportunistic infections and tropical diseases) — see the Fever in the returning traveller card.
