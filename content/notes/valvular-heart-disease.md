background: |+
  Structural or functional abnormality of a heart valve causing stenosis (impaired opening, obstructing forward flow), regurgitation (impaired closure, allowing backward flow), or both. The left-sided valves (aortic, mitral) cause the large majority of clinically significant disease and symptoms.

  - **Aortic stenosis**: the commonest valve disease needing intervention in the developed world — usually **degenerative/calcific** in older patients, or related to a **congenital bicuspid aortic valve** presenting earlier (40s–60s); rheumatic disease is a less common cause now in the UK.
  - **Aortic regurgitation**: from valve leaflet disease (bicuspid valve, endocarditis, rheumatic disease) or **aortic root dilation** (hypertension, Marfan's, aortic dissection, ankylosing spondylitis, syphilis — root dilation pulls the leaflets apart even if they are structurally normal).
  - **Mitral stenosis**: **rheumatic heart disease is by far the commonest cause** worldwide and historically in the UK — now less common with falling rheumatic fever rates, but still seen, particularly in patients who grew up where rheumatic fever remains prevalent.
  - **Mitral regurgitation**: **primary** (the valve itself is abnormal — myxomatous/prolapse, rheumatic, endocarditis, papillary muscle rupture post-MI) versus **secondary/functional** (the valve is structurally normal but fails to coapt because the LV has dilated/remodelled, e.g. after MI or in dilated cardiomyopathy) — this distinction matters because the treatment approach differs (see Treatment).
diagnosis: |+
  - **Aortic stenosis**: classic triad of **syncope (exertional), angina, and dyspnoea ("SAD")** as the valve narrows and the heart can no longer increase output to meet demand — exertional syncope in particular is an ominous symptom. **Ejection systolic murmur**, loudest at the right upper sternal edge, radiating to the carotids, with a **slow-rising ("anacrotic") pulse** and a **soft or absent second heart sound** (as the calcified valve stops moving) in severe disease.
  - **Aortic regurgitation**: can be asymptomatic for years (the LV compensates by dilating) before presenting with exertional dyspnoea/heart failure. **Early diastolic murmur**, loudest at the left sternal edge, leaning forward in expiration, with a **collapsing ("water-hammer") pulse** and a **wide pulse pressure** — many eponymous peripheral signs exist (Corrigan's, de Musset's, Quincke's) but are now mostly of historical/exam interest rather than practical bedside importance.
  - **Mitral stenosis**: dyspnoea (from raised left atrial/pulmonary pressure), fatigue, and a predisposition to **atrial fibrillation** (from left atrial dilation/strain) — which itself can precipitate acute decompensation or embolic stroke. **Mid-diastolic, low-pitched "rumbling" murmur** best heard at the apex in the left lateral position, with a **loud first heart sound** and an **opening snap**; a **malar flush** can occur in severe disease (low cardiac output).
  - **Mitral regurgitation**: can be asymptomatic for a long time before causing exertional dyspnoea and fatigue as the LV decompensates. **Pansystolic murmur**, loudest at the apex, radiating to the axilla.
  - Always examine for signs of the **underlying cause** (features of Marfan's or ankylosing spondylitis with AR; signs of prior rheumatic fever/endocarditis; evidence of a prior MI with secondary MR) alongside the murmur itself.
investigations: |+
  - **Transthoracic echocardiography** is the key investigation for every suspected valve lesion — confirms the diagnosis, defines severity (see below), and assesses the knock-on effect on chamber size/function (LV size and function, left atrial size, pulmonary artery pressure).
  - **Severe aortic stenosis**: **valve area <1.0 cm², mean pressure gradient ≥40 mmHg, peak velocity ≥4.0 m/s** (any one of these, interpreted together with flow status — a low-flow state can make the gradient look falsely lower than the valve area suggests, needing further assessment such as dobutamine stress echo).
  - **Severe mitral stenosis**: **valve area <1.0 cm²**, mean diastolic gradient **>10 mmHg**.
  - Severity of regurgitant lesions (aortic or mitral regurgitation) is graded by a combination of qualitative and quantitative echo criteria (jet size/area, vena contracta, regurgitant volume/fraction) rather than a single number — reported by the echocardiographer as mild/moderate/severe.
  - **ECG**: left ventricular hypertrophy (aortic stenosis, aortic/mitral regurgitation), AF or left atrial enlargement/"P mitrale" (mitral stenosis).
  - **Cardiac catheterisation** is reserved for cases where echo findings are inconclusive, or routinely before surgery to assess the coronary arteries in an older patient.
treatment: |+
  - **Aortic stenosis**: **no medical therapy alters the natural history** — management is surveillance (serial echo, interval depends on severity) until intervention is needed. **Valve replacement is indicated for symptomatic severe AS** (regardless of LV function), or for asymptomatic severe AS with LV systolic dysfunction or a markedly abnormal response to exercise testing. **Choice of TAVI (transcatheter aortic valve implantation) versus SAVR (surgical aortic valve replacement)** is guided mainly by **age and surgical risk** — broadly, younger/lower-risk patients favour SAVR (better long-term valve durability data), older/higher-risk patients favour TAVI, with many patients falling into a shared-decision zone assessed by the heart team.
  - **Aortic regurgitation**: surgery (valve repair or replacement) for **symptomatic severe AR regardless of LV function**, or asymptomatic severe AR with LV dilation/dysfunction on surveillance imaging. ACE inhibitors/ARBs can help symptomatically but do not replace surgery when it is indicated.
  - **Mitral stenosis**: **percutaneous balloon mitral valvotomy** is preferred where the valve morphology is favourable (pliable, non-calcified, minimal regurgitation) — if not suitable, surgical repair or replacement. Rate control and anticoagulation for associated AF (mitral stenosis with AF is itself sufficient indication for anticoagulation, independent of CHA2DS2-VASc, given the particularly high stroke risk).
  - **Mitral regurgitation**: **primary MR** — surgical repair (preferred over replacement where feasible, with better outcomes) for symptomatic severe MR, or asymptomatic severe MR with LV dilation/dysfunction; percutaneous edge-to-edge repair is an option for selected patients unsuitable for surgery. **Secondary/functional MR** — optimise guideline-directed heart failure therapy first (see the Heart failure factfile), since treating the underlying LV dysfunction can itself reduce the regurgitation; intervention (surgical or percutaneous) is reserved for those remaining severely symptomatic despite optimal medical therapy.
  - **Endocarditis prophylaxis**: NICE does not recommend routine antibiotic prophylaxis for dental or other procedures based on valve disease alone (see the Infective endocarditis factfile, Acute medicine) — good oral hygiene and prompt treatment of infection are advised instead.
protocols: |+
  - **Acute severe regurgitation** (papillary muscle rupture causing acute severe MR post-MI, or acute AR from endocarditis/dissection) presents as acute pulmonary oedema and/or cardiogenic shock and needs emergency assessment for urgent surgery — see the Cardiogenic shock and Heart failure cards; it is poorly tolerated because the LV/LA have not had time to adapt, unlike chronic regurgitation.
  - **Severe aortic stenosis with syncope or angina** should prompt urgent cardiology referral rather than routine outpatient follow-up, given the risk of sudden cardiac death once symptoms develop.
  - **New decompensation in known valve disease** (new AF, infection, anaemia, or non-cardiac surgery) often precipitates acute symptoms in previously compensated disease — treat the precipitant while reassessing whether the underlying valve disease itself now needs intervention.
  - See the Infective endocarditis factfile (Acute medicine) for prosthetic valve endocarditis and the general approach to endocarditis in valve disease.
