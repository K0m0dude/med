background: |+
  A large, heterogeneous group of conditions causing inflammation and/or fibrosis of the lung interstitium (the tissue between the alveolar epithelium and capillary endothelium), restricting lung expansion and impairing gas transfer. Broadly split by cause.

  Idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis (IPF)

  - The commonest idiopathic ILD — progressive fibrosis of unknown cause, typically in patients **>60 years**, more often men, strongly associated with smoking. Median survival historically around 3–5 years from diagnosis, though this varies widely and antifibrotic treatment has improved the outlook for some.
  - Pathologically and radiologically defined by a **usual interstitial pneumonia (UIP) pattern** — the diagnostic hallmark (see Investigations).

  Connective tissue disease-associated ILD

  - Rheumatoid arthritis, systemic sclerosis, Sjögren's syndrome, polymyositis/dermatomyositis and SLE can all cause ILD, sometimes preceding the joint/skin features of the underlying disease. Often has a **non-specific interstitial pneumonia (NSIP)** pattern rather than UIP, which carries a different (generally better) prognosis and different treatment approach (immunosuppression may help, unlike IPF).

  Hypersensitivity pneumonitis (extrinsic allergic alveolitis)

  - An immune reaction to an inhaled organic antigen. Classic examples: **"farmer's lung"** (mouldy hay, thermophilic actinomycetes), **"bird fancier's lung"** (avian proteins in droppings/feathers), and reactions to mould-contaminated ventilation systems. Acute, subacute and chronic forms exist — chronic disease can become indistinguishable from fibrotic ILD if the trigger isn't identified and removed.

  Sarcoidosis

  - A multisystem granulomatous disease of unknown cause, most often affecting the lungs and intrathoracic lymph nodes, typically in young adults. Often found incidentally; can also cause skin, eye, cardiac and neurological disease.

  Drug-induced and occupational ILD

  - Drugs: amiodarone, methotrexate, nitrofurantoin, bleomycin and other chemotherapy agents. Occupational causes (asbestosis, silicosis) are covered in the dedicated Occupational lung disease card.
diagnosis: |+
  - Progressive exertional breathlessness and a persistent **dry cough**, developing over months (IPF, chronic hypersensitivity pneumonitis, sarcoidosis) or more acutely (acute hypersensitivity pneumonitis, drug-induced). Fatigue and weight loss can occur.
  - **Bibasal fine end-inspiratory crackles** ("Velcro crackles") are classic for IPF and other fibrotic ILD. **Finger clubbing** is common in IPF (and asbestosis) but not typical of sarcoidosis.
  - **Sarcoidosis** can present very differently: asymptomatic bilateral hilar lymphadenopathy on an incidental CXR, or **Löfgren's syndrome** (bilateral hilar lymphadenopathy + erythema nodosum + polyarthralgia — an acute presentation with a good prognosis, often resolving spontaneously), uveitis, hypercalcaemia (granulomas produce active vitamin D), or rarely cardiac (arrhythmia, heart block) or neurological (cranial nerve palsies, especially facial nerve) involvement.
  - **Acute hypersensitivity pneumonitis**: fever, cough and breathlessness **4–12 hours after antigen exposure**, improving over days once away from the source — an important pattern to ask about directly (occupational and hobby history, including pets and mould exposure), since it's easily mistaken for recurrent chest infections.
  - Ask specifically about **drug history** (amiodarone, methotrexate, nitrofurantoin), **occupational and environmental exposures**, and **connective tissue disease symptoms** (joint pain/swelling, Raynaud's, skin tightening, dry eyes/mouth, muscle weakness) in every new ILD presentation.
investigations: |+
  - **HRCT (high-resolution CT) is the key investigation.** A **UIP pattern** — basal- and peripheral-predominant reticulation, **honeycombing**, and **traction bronchiectasis**, with no features suggesting an alternative diagnosis — is sufficiently characteristic that a **confident IPF diagnosis can sometimes be made on HRCT alone**, without biopsy, especially when combined with a fitting clinical picture and no identified cause. An atypical or inconsistent pattern prompts MDT discussion ± lung biopsy.
  - **Pulmonary function tests**: a **restrictive pattern** (reduced FVC and TLC, with a preserved or increased FEV1/FVC ratio) and a **reduced gas transfer (TLCO/KCO)** — the TLCO often falls before symptoms or a restrictive spirometry pattern are obvious, and serial TLCO/FVC trends are used to monitor progression.
  - **Bloods**: connective tissue disease/autoimmune screen (ANA, rheumatoid factor, anti-CCP, ENA panel, myositis antibodies) in anyone without an obvious cause — a positive result changes the likely ILD subtype and treatment. **ACE level** is sometimes raised in sarcoidosis but is neither sensitive nor specific enough to diagnose or exclude it. Serum **calcium** (hypercalcaemia in sarcoidosis).
  - **Sarcoidosis**: tissue diagnosis (transbronchial or lymph node biopsy showing non-caseating granulomas) is usually needed unless Löfgren's syndrome is clinically obvious, in which case biopsy can often be avoided.
  - **Hypersensitivity pneumonitis**: specific IgG precipitins to the suspected antigen support the diagnosis alongside a fitting exposure history and HRCT pattern (which can show air-trapping/mosaic attenuation, especially in subacute/chronic disease).
  - Multidisciplinary team (MDT) discussion — combining clinical, radiological and (where available) pathological findings — is the standard of care for reaching a confident ILD diagnosis, rather than any single test in isolation.
treatment: |+
  - **IPF**: **antifibrotic therapy** — **pirfenidone** or **nintedanib** — slows the rate of decline in lung function; neither reverses existing fibrosis or restores lost function. Supportive care: pulmonary rehabilitation, oxygen therapy for hypoxia, and advance care planning given the generally progressive course. **Lung transplantation** is considered in suitable younger patients with progressive disease. Treat/prevent gastro-oesophageal reflux (thought to contribute via microaspiration) and ensure vaccination (influenza, pneumococcal). **Immunosuppression (e.g. prednisolone) is not used in IPF** — it does not improve outcomes and can be harmful, unlike in other fibrotic ILD subtypes.
  - **Connective tissue disease-associated ILD and other inflammatory ILD** (including NSIP): immunosuppression (corticosteroids, often with a steroid-sparing agent such as mycophenolate or azathioprine) is more likely to help here than in IPF, since inflammation plays a larger role; manage alongside rheumatology.
  - **Hypersensitivity pneumonitis**: **remove/avoid the causative antigen** — the single most important step, and essential even if other treatment is given. Corticosteroids for symptomatic acute/subacute disease. Established chronic fibrotic change may not reverse even with antigen avoidance.
  - **Sarcoidosis**: many cases (especially Löfgren's syndrome) resolve spontaneously and need only monitoring and NSAIDs for symptom control. **Corticosteroids** are used for significant or progressive pulmonary disease, or for extrapulmonary organ-threatening disease (cardiac, neurological, ocular, hypercalcaemia) — the threshold to treat is based on the organs and severity involved, not simply having a diagnosis of sarcoidosis.
  - General supportive measures across all fibrotic ILD: pulmonary rehabilitation, oxygen assessment, vaccination, smoking cessation, and early palliative care input for symptom management (especially breathlessness) as disease progresses.
protocols: |+
  - **Acute exacerbation of IPF**: unexplained, significant worsening of breathlessness over days to weeks, with new bilateral opacification on imaging not fully explained by infection, heart failure or PE — a recognised, often fatal complication with no proven specific treatment beyond excluding and treating reversible causes (infection, heart failure, PE) and supportive/palliative care; high-dose corticosteroids are often tried, though evidence for benefit is limited.
  - **Acute hypersensitivity pneumonitis** causing significant hypoxia needs urgent removal from the environment, oxygen, and corticosteroids — delay in recognising the exposure history risks ongoing harm and progression to chronic fibrotic disease.
  - **Cardiac or neurological sarcoidosis** (new arrhythmia, heart block, cranial nerve palsy, other neurological deficit) needs urgent specialist assessment and usually prompt immunosuppression — these are the sarcoidosis presentations most likely to cause serious harm if treatment is delayed.
  - New or worsening hypoxia in known fibrotic ILD should prompt a search for a treatable superimposed cause (infection, pneumothorax, heart failure, PE) before being attributed to disease progression alone.
