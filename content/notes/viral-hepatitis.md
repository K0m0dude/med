background: |+
  Inflammation of the liver caused by hepatotropic viruses. **A and E are faecal–oral and (mostly) acute and self-limiting; B, C and D are blood-borne and can cause chronic infection, cirrhosis and hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC).**

  - **Hepatitis A**: faecal–oral (food, water, men who have sex with men, travel). Acute, never chronic. **Vaccine** available.
  - **Hepatitis B (HBV)**: blood, sexual and **vertical (mother to baby)** transmission. **The risk of chronicity depends on age at infection**: about **90% of perinatal infections** become chronic, vs **<5% of adult infections**.
  - **Hepatitis C (HCV)**: blood-borne (**injecting drug use**, historical transfusion/blood products, unsafe medical procedures). **About 75% develop chronic infection.** No vaccine.
  - **Hepatitis D (HDV)**: a **defective RNA virus that needs HBsAg to replicate**; occurs as **co-infection** (with acute HBV) or **superinfection** (on chronic HBV, which is more severe).
  - **Hepatitis E (HEV)**: faecal–oral. **Genotype 3 (zoonotic; pork/game in the UK)** usually causes mild disease but can become **chronic in immunosuppressed patients**; **genotypes 1/2 (water-borne, Asia/Africa)** cause outbreaks with **high mortality in pregnancy**.

  > **Microbiology and pathology**
  >
  > - **HBV** is a partially double-stranded **DNA hepadnavirus** that replicates through an **RNA intermediate by reverse transcription**. The persistent template is **cccDNA** in the hepatocyte nucleus, which is why antivirals suppress but rarely cure infection. HBV can also **integrate into the host genome** and (with the **HBx** protein and chronic inflammation) drives HCC, **even without cirrhosis**.
  > - **Hepatocyte injury is largely immune-mediated, not directly cytopathic**: **CD8⁺ T cells and NK cells** kill infected hepatocytes. A vigorous response clears acute infection (and can cause fulminant hepatitis); a weak, tolerant or exhausted response permits chronic infection. Histology: **ground-glass hepatocytes** (HBsAg accumulation in the ER), **Councilman bodies** (apoptotic hepatocytes), interface hepatitis, bridging necrosis, and fibrosis.
  > - **Chronic HBV phases**: **immune tolerant** (HBeAg⁺, very high DNA, normal ALT) → **immune clearance/HBeAg-positive hepatitis** (raised ALT) → **inactive carrier** (HBeAg⁻, anti-HBe⁺, low DNA, normal ALT) → **HBeAg-negative chronic hepatitis** (precore mutants; fluctuating ALT). Immunosuppression can **reactivate** HBV.
  > - **HCV** is a positive-sense **RNA flavivirus** with high mutation rates (quasispecies and six genotypes), which helps it evade immunity. It replicates in the cytoplasm with no DNA intermediate, so **it can be fully cured**. Direct-acting antivirals target **NS3/4A (protease), NS5A and NS5B (polymerase)**.
  > - **Extrahepatic disease**: **HBV** — polyarteritis nodosa and membranous nephropathy; **HCV** — **mixed cryoglobulinaemia**, membranoproliferative glomerulonephritis, porphyria cutanea tarda, lichen planus and B-cell lymphoma.
diagnosis: |+
  - **Acute hepatitis**: **prodrome** (malaise, anorexia, nausea, low-grade fever, RUQ discomfort; arthralgia and urticaria in HBV), then **jaundice, dark urine and pale stool**. Many cases (especially HCV and children with HAV) are **asymptomatic**.
  - **Acute liver failure** (jaundice, **encephalopathy and coagulopathy (INR >1.5)** in a patient with no prior liver disease) is rare but life-threatening: HBV (including reactivation), HAV, HEV (especially in pregnancy), paracetamol and other causes.
  - **Chronic hepatitis (B, C, D)**: often silent; discovered by abnormal LFTs, screening, or at the stage of **cirrhosis** (ascites, varices, encephalopathy, HCC). Ask about risk factors: injecting drug use, sexual history, country of birth, blood product exposure, tattoos, family history.
  - **Examination** for stigmata of chronic liver disease, splenomegaly, and (in acute disease) tender hepatomegaly.
investigations: |+
  - **LFTs** (ALT/AST typically in the **hundreds to thousands in acute hepatitis**), bilirubin, **INR** (the key marker of severity), albumin, FBC, glucose; exclude other causes (paracetamol, drugs, autoimmune, alcohol, Wilson's, ischaemia).
  - **First-line serology**: **HBsAg**, **anti-HBc IgM and total**, **anti-HCV antibody with reflex HCV RNA PCR**, **anti-HAV IgM**, **anti-HEV IgM and HEV RNA**; **HIV test** (shared risk). In HBsAg-positive patients: **HBeAg, anti-HBe, HBV DNA**, and **anti-HDV** (and HDV RNA).
  - **Liver ultrasound** (with Doppler), and **non-invasive fibrosis assessment (FibroScan/elastography, ELF)**. **Cirrhosis or high-risk HBV: HCC surveillance with ultrasound ± AFP every 6 months.**

  | Pattern | Interpretation |
  |---|---|
  | HBsAg +, anti-HBc IgM + | Acute HBV |
  | HBsAg +, anti-HBc IgG +, IgM −, persisting >6 months | Chronic HBV |
  | HBsAg −, anti-HBs +, anti-HBc − | Vaccinated (immune) |
  | HBsAg −, anti-HBs +, anti-HBc + | Resolved natural infection (immune) |
  | HBsAg −, anti-HBc + only | Window period, resolved infection with waning anti-HBs, or **occult HBV** (reactivation risk on immunosuppression) |

  - **HBeAg positivity = high viral replication and infectivity**; **HBV DNA** = viral load and treatment indication. **HCV RNA positive = active infection**; anti-HCV alone can reflect past, cleared infection.
treatment: |+
  - **Hepatitis A and E (acute)**: **supportive care**; monitor for liver failure. **Chronic HEV in the immunosuppressed**: reduce immunosuppression and consider **ribavirin**. Hepatitis A: notifiable; vaccination and immunoglobulin for contacts as per UKHSA advice.
  - **Acute hepatitis B**: supportive in most; **antivirals (tenofovir or entecavir)** for severe acute disease/acute liver failure or prolonged illness, on specialist advice.
  - **Chronic hepatitis B**: treat according to **HBV DNA, ALT and fibrosis stage** (and in cirrhosis regardless of DNA). **Long-term nucleos(t)ide analogues (tenofovir or entecavir)** have a **high barrier to resistance** and are the mainstay of modern practice (EASL/BASL); **NICE CG165 (2013, updated 2017)** still lists **peginterferon alfa-2a for 48 weeks** as first-line in compensated disease and tenofovir/entecavir otherwise, so follow the specialist/local pathway. **Pre-treat screen all patients before chemotherapy, rituximab or other immunosuppression** (HBsAg, anti-HBc) and give **prophylactic tenofovir/entecavir** to prevent reactivation.
  - **Chronic hepatitis C: direct-acting antivirals (DAAs)** for **everyone with detectable HCV RNA** — pan-genotypic regimens (for example **sofosbuvir–velpatasvir for 12 weeks, or glecaprevir–pibrentasvir for 8 weeks**; regimens vary with genotype, cirrhosis and prior treatment), with **cure (SVR12) in >95%**. Treat in people who inject drugs too (reinfection is possible; pair with harm reduction). England is working towards **HCV elimination**.
  - **Hepatitis D**: specialist management (**peginterferon**, newer agents); treat the underlying HBV.
  - **Prevention**: **HAV and HBV vaccines** (HBV is part of the UK infant schedule and offered to at-risk groups); **HBV vaccine plus HBIG within 24 hours for babies born to HBsAg-positive mothers**; maternal **tenofovir in the third trimester** if HBV DNA is high; **post-exposure prophylaxis after needlestick/sexual exposure** (HBV vaccine ± HBIG depending on immunity; baseline and follow-up HCV/HIV testing). Harm reduction (needle exchange, opioid substitution) and screening of at-risk groups.
protocols: |+
  > Red flag: **acute hepatitis with new confusion, jaundice and a rising INR** = acute liver failure — urgent referral to a liver unit for transplant assessment (King's College criteria), avoid sedatives, and treat as a medical emergency.

  - **HBV reactivation** (rising HBV DNA and ALT after chemotherapy, biologics or high-dose steroids, or after anti-HCV DAA therapy in co-infected patients) can be fatal: **screen before immunosuppression and give prophylaxis**.
  - **Hepatitis E in pregnancy (genotypes 1/2)** carries high maternal mortality; seek urgent specialist obstetric/hepatology care.
  - **Needlestick or mucosal exposure**: wash/irrigate, assess source and recipient status, give HBV vaccine ± HBIG and arrange HIV and HCV follow-up (see the local occupational health/PEP policy).
  - **Notification**: acute hepatitis A, B and C (and others) are **notifiable** to the proper officer (health protection team).
